Lodge Reference

Key Passages for Masonic Ritual & Study

The Volume of the Sacred Law is one of the Three Great Lights of Freemasonry, and lies open upon the altar of every regular Lodge. The following passages bear special significance in the rituals, symbols, and teachings of the Craft — arranged here by degree for the instruction of the Brethren.
I°

Entered Apprentice

The First Degree — From Darkness to Light

Let There Be Light
Genesis 1:1-3
The candidate receives light — the central moment of initiation. From darkness to illumination, the first step on the path of the Craft.
The Plucking Off of the Shoe
Ruth 4:7-8
An ancient mode of confirmation — a token of sincerity. The rite of discalceation recalls the candidate’s preparation for initiation.
Behold, How Good
Psalms 133:1-3
The psalm of Brotherly Love, traditionally recited at the opening of the Lodge. Unity is the first tenet of Freemasonry.
The Plumb Line
Amos 7:7-8
The working tool of the Entered Apprentice. The Lord sets a plumb line in the midst of His people — a symbol of moral rectitude and upright conduct.
Ask, and It Shall Be Given
Matthew 7:7-8
The candidate must seek knowledge of his own volition. None are compelled — the door opens only to him who knocks.
II°

Fellow Craft

The Second Degree — The Winding Staircase

Shibboleth
Judges 12:1-6
The password of the Second Degree. The Ephraimites could not frame to pronounce it right — a token of recognition among the initiated.
The Building of the Temple
1 Kings 6:1-8
The Fellow Craft ascends the winding staircase of Solomon’s Temple. These chapters are the heart of Craft Masonry — the sacred architecture of the soul.
The Two Great Pillars
1 Kings 7:13-22
Jachin and Boaz — “He shall establish” and “In it is strength.” The twin pillars at the porch of the Temple, through which the Fellow Craft symbolically passes.
Pillars of the Porch
2 Chronicles 3:15-17
The parallel account of the pillars, with their chapiters of lilies, pomegranates, and network — symbols of peace, plenty, and the interconnection of all Brethren.
Remember Thy Creator
Ecclesiastes 12:1-7
A meditation on mortality read in the Second Degree. The silver cord, the golden bowl, the pitcher at the fountain — all allegories of the frailty of life.
III°

Master Mason

The Third Degree — The Legend of the Master Builder

Tubal-Cain
Genesis 4:22
The first artificer in brass and iron — an instructor of every craftsman. Tubal-Cain is the pass-grip word of the Master Mason degree.
The Preparation of Materials
1 Kings 5:1-18
Solomon’s covenant with Hiram of Tyre. The cedar, the stone, the levy of workers — the great labour begins. In the Third Degree, the legend of the Master Builder unfolds.
Solomon Prepares to Build
2 Chronicles 2:1-16
The census of the workers, the request for a cunning man — the Temple as the supreme symbol of the Master’s work, ordered from chaos.
To Every Thing a Season
Ecclesiastes 3:1-8
A time to be born and a time to die, a time to build up — the great allegory of cycles. The Master Mason contemplates the hourglass.
In the Beginning Was the Word
John 1:1-5
The Logos — the sacred Word, lost and sought. The light shineth in darkness, and the darkness comprehended it not. The central mystery of the Third Degree.
△

General

Passages of Universal Masonic Significance

The Sacrifice of Isaac on Mount Moriah
Genesis 22:1-14
Mount Moriah — the same ground upon which Solomon would raise the Temple. The supreme trial of faith, the foundation stone of the sacred hill.
Jacob's Ladder
Genesis 28:10-22
The ladder reaching to heaven, with angels ascending and descending. In Masonic symbolism: Faith, Hope, and Charity — the three principal rounds.
The Burning Bush
Exodus 3:1-6
Holy ground — put off thy shoes. The sacred fire that burns but does not consume, a symbol of the Divine presence in the Lodge.
Who Shall Ascend
Psalms 24:3-5
He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart — the qualifications for admission to the holy hill, and to the Lodge.
Except the Lord Build the House
Psalms 127:1
The builders labour in vain without the Grand Architect. The eternal lesson of the Craft: all labour must be consecrated.
The Search for Wisdom
Proverbs 2:1-9
Seek her as silver, search for her as hid treasures — the Craft is a system of morality, veiled in allegory and illustrated by symbols.
Happy Is the Man That Findeth Wisdom
Proverbs 3:13-20
She is more precious than rubies. By wisdom hath the Lord founded the earth — the Great Architect of the Universe, the supreme Masonic conception of the Divine.
The Cornerstone
Isaiah 28:16-17
A tried stone, a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation — judgment laid to the line, and righteousness to the plummet. The working tools made divine.
